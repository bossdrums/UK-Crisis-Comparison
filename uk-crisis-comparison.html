03 — Prices

Inflation

The Ukraine war drove inflation to a 40-year high. The Iran conflict threatens a second wave just as prices were settling back towards target.

04 — Energy

Energy Price Shocks

Each crisis had a completely different energy story, from collapsing demand to supply-side shocks.

Wholesale Price Changes
Approximate % change in UK energy prices during each crisis
📉
COVID-19

Oil prices briefly went negative in April 2020. Demand collapsed as lockdowns shut down travel and industry worldwide.

🔥
Ukraine War

Wholesale gas surged by up to 300%. Russia's supply cuts triggered the worst European energy crisis in decades. UK household bills doubled.

⛽
Iran War

Strait of Hormuz closure removed ~10% of global oil supply. UK wholesale gas up 75% in one month. Diesel prices up 20% within weeks.

05 — Fiscal Response

Government Spending & Debt

COVID triggered the largest peacetime fiscal intervention in UK history. Subsequent crises have landed on an already stretched public balance sheet.

Direct Government Spending

Context: COVID cost the UK government an estimated £310-410 billion, equivalent to roughly £4,600 to £6,100 per person. Government borrowing hit £313 billion in 2020/21 alone, a level not seen outside of wartime. The UK is now navigating new shocks with debt already near 100% of GDP.
06 — Overall

Comparative Severity

Each crisis caused damage in different dimensions. This radar shows the relative severity across six key economic measures.

The big picture: COVID was a heart attack, sudden and dramatic with massive emergency spending. The Ukraine war was a slow-burning infection, eroding living standards through persistent inflation. The Iran conflict is an energy supply shock landing on an economy still recovering from the cumulative damage of the previous two.
